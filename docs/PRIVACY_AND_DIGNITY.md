# Privacy and dignity

Recipients do not prove or publicly describe poverty. The product says *recipient*, *community member*, *food support* and *rescue request*. A recipient can enable a public alias; donors see that alias and an approximate area, not a phone number or precise address. Collection points provide a handover option that does not expose a home.

The system retains a minimal fictional account identity for accountability, visible to coordinator/admin roles. This is pseudonymity, not full anonymity. Exact coordinates are used internally for demo distance calculation but are not returned in listing responses as public pickup coordinates. In-app messages avoid direct phone exchange.

No NIC numbers, payment details, medical records or hardship evidence are stored. A real pilot needs purpose/retention definitions, consent and notice, access logging, deletion and correction procedures, incident handling, vendor review, encryption/key management and a Sri Lankan privacy-law assessment by qualified specialists.

Design choices should be tested with recipients and community partners: privacy language that sounds protective to a designer can still create stigma or uncertainty in use.
