# Product vision

Sri Lanka has suitable food that becomes waste while nearby people and community organisations could use food support. Coordination is difficult: availability windows are short, handling history matters, logistics are local, and support must not force people to publicly prove hardship.

PlateBridge LK proposes a **food-rescue coordination network**, not an unrestricted leftovers marketplace. Donors describe food and handling; a transparent screening model routes green items to matching, amber items to a coordinator, and red items away from human redistribution. Recipients browse without disclosing circumstances, use a public alias, and select collection or volunteer delivery. Pickup codes and audit events support accountability.

Target participants are households, bakeries, hotels, restaurants, canteens, event organisers, community organisations, recipients, trained volunteers and regional coordinators. Dignity is a product constraint: a request for food support should reveal no more identity or circumstance than necessary.

The long-term vision is a locally governed network of verified food partners, professional safety protocols, reliable collection points, trained volunteers and accountable waste-routing integrations. The product can support coordination and measurement; it cannot substitute for competent safety, legal or public-health governance.
